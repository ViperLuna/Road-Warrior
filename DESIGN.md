# Road Warrior - Design Doc (living spec)

A Mini Motorways-style source/destination road-building game. Vanilla JS + Canvas, no build step, deployed to GitHub Pages via Actions.

## Core loop
- Fixed-size grid map with randomly generated water (river / pond / lake). Land must stay connected (flood-fill check); start area is open land.
- Houses (1 tile) and destinations (2x2: 2 building tiles + 2 parking tiles, entrance on the lot) come in matching colors. Buildings can be **rotated, never moved**. One exit side each; a road touching the exit auto-connects.
- Player drags to build roads (left-click / create button), right-click / destroy button to demolish. Drag-build any time.
- Each house owns **1 car** of its color. Car picks a random same-color destination, drives there, returns. A full round trip = **1 trip**.
- Destination holds max **4** cars (2 lot tiles x 2 bays, any gate reaches any bay). House reserves a slot on departure; if none free, the car waits in the house queue. Marker per inbound car shown on the destination.
- Spawn logic must keep houses:destinations balanced (about 4 houses per destination).

## Controls
- **Mouse:** left = current tool (Build/Demolish/Move), right = demolish, middle-drag or Space+left-drag = pan, wheel = zoom at cursor. Keys: B / D / M switch tool.
- **Touch:** toolbar toggles Build / Demolish / Move; one finger uses the lit tool, two fingers always pan + pinch-zoom.
- Drags are walked tile-by-tile (4-connected), so fast swipes never leave gaps. Seed is in the URL (`#seed=123`) so a map can be replayed/shared.

## Tuning (config/tuning.json)
All gameplay numbers live in `config/tuning.json`, read at startup (defaults in `js/tuning.js`): lane speeds (**`twoLane` / `fourLane` separate**, tiles/s) with turn/driveway/lot factors, **traffic light** green/yellow/all-red seconds, car accel/braking/min gap, parked time, trip reward, home cooldown. Colors live in `config/colors.json`.

## Simulation model
- Every movement is a Bezier "piece" (road tile in-side -> out-side, driveway, lot in/out) offset into the right-hand lane (`js/lanes.js`). Pathfinding is A* over (tile, entry side) with small turn penalties and no U-turns (`js/pathfind.js`).
- A car's route is a list of pieces. A piece the map no longer supports (demolished road, rotated building) is drawn as a faded **ghost** until the car is done with it; no extra bookkeeping.
- Dev hotkeys: **R** rotate hovered building, **P** spawn an extra pair (temporary, until real spawning in M4). Click/tap a building to rotate it.

## Specials in play (M5)
- **Placing:** tap a special's pill in the top bar (Roundabout / Traffic light), then tap an intersection (a road tile with 3+ connections). Demolishing the tile returns the special. Both are rewards and also sold in the Shop.
- **Stop signs:** plain 4-ways and the stem of a T make every car come to a full stop (`junctions.stopSeconds`, default 1.2 s) before going by right of way. Through traffic on a T doesn't stop. This is what makes busy plain junctions a bottleneck, so the specials matter.
- **Roundabout:** cars circulate counter-clockwise (US) around a central island; entrants yield to circulating cars. Best throughput under load; in an overloaded crossroads it keeps the same trips as a plain junction with far shorter worst waits as traffic climbs.
- **Traffic light:** fixed timer from `trafficLight.*` in tuning.json, two phases by axis (N/S then E/W), all lights on one shared clock. Right turn on red is allowed when nothing conflicts. Lights can't sit on neighboring tiles (no room to queue between them), and a car won't enter a plain junction if the light just beyond it isn't green (so nobody parks inside an intersection waiting for the next light). Lights are a stability tool: under heavy load a plain junction can collapse into long stalls while a lit one stays steady, but throughput is lower than a roundabout.
- **One-way streets:** a permanent unlock (reward or $120 in the Shop) that adds a toolbar toggle (key **5** on the number row; the letter O now belongs to the roundabout). With it on, dragging along roads makes each street one-way in the direction you drag (white lane dashes + chevrons); with it off, dragging along roads makes them two-way again. Stored per edge as blocked exits; pathfinding refuses to drive against an arrow and rebuilding a tile clears stale flags.
- **Hills + tunnels:** about 70% of maps have rocky hills. Roads and buildings can't go on hills; a **tunnel** is dug into the hill ONE BLOCK AT A TIME, each block using one tunnel piece ($60 each, or a reward of 4); see "Tunnels: dug one block at a time" at the end of this file. Cars vanish inside and reappear at a mouth. Tunnels are only offered on maps that have hills, and across-water spawning also considers hill crossings.
- **Smarter spawning across water:** houses/pairs only spawn on the far bank when the player could connect them: an unused bridge in hand or already built over that gap, and enough roads for both land stretches plus a reserve. More spare bridges = more separation.

## Highways (M6)
- **A reward (10 pieces) or $90 in the Shop.** Tap the Highway pill to switch the Build tool to 4-lane road; dragging over a street widens it in place (one highway piece in, one street piece refunded). Demolishing returns the right kind of piece. Bridges and tunnels inherit the lane count of the road you're dragging.
- **Lanes:** two per direction (inner/outer, 0.11/0.33 from the median). Each car picks a lane per trip, so there is no mid-road lane changing. Speeds are separate in tuning.json (`speed.twoLane` 2.4, `speed.fourLane` 3.6).
- **Joining streets and highways:** every tile edge has a lane count = the smaller of its two neighbors (2 where it meets a driveway or gate). A highway tile next to a street tapers inside itself, drawn as a flare; where two lanes merge into one the tile acts as a junction and the usual conflict logic does the merging. A side street onto a highway is just a normal T.
- **Slowing for junctions:** everyone eases to `speed.junctionApproach` (2.2) when approaching a junction, so a fast road never outruns its stopping distance. This one change removed ~96% of highway collisions.
- **Roundabouts only fit on regular streets.** Traffic lights work on either.
- **Overpass (placeable special, $80):** put it on a 4-way where a highway crosses a street and nothing else joins. The tile becomes two independent layers: the street runs underneath, the highway passes over, no turns, no interaction. In the crossing test it moves ~12% more trips than the same crossing at ground level, with shorter waits. Cars are drawn under the deck, then the deck, then everyone else. Only offered as a reward once you own highways.
- **Ramps:** side streets joining a highway at ground level serve as on/off ramps (each is just a normal street piece).
- **Known rough edge:** in dense layouts with many highway/street mixes, car bodies can briefly graze (about 1 second per 2.7 hours of simulated driving in stress tests).

## Roads
- Tile holds either 2-lane or 4-lane road (one-way is a build toggle). Edges stay visually clear except where road ends / driveways meet.
- US right-hand traffic.
- Initial inventory: 20 two-lane pieces + 1 bridge (unused until the city grows toward water). First pair is spawned on open land within range of the piece count.
- **Demolish always refunds the piece immediately.** A demolished road in use by a car persists as a **ghost road** until its cars finish, even if rebuilt over; new cars use the new path. Rotating a connected building leaves a ghost connector the same way.

## Intersections (implemented in `js/junction.js` + `js/cars.js`)
- **Conflicts are geometric:** two movements conflict if their lane curves come within a car width (`cars.conflictDistance`). Crossings, merges and left turns fall out of the geometry; a right turn into your own lane conflicts only with traffic merging into that same lane.
- **T-junction:** through road beats the stem; left turns yield to oncoming straight. **4-way:** first-come-first-served, ties yield to the car on the right. A car already past its stop line always finishes.
- **Never wait inside a junction:** a car won't enter unless the whole chain of junction/lot pieces ahead is free and there is room beyond the last one (no box-blocking). Cars inside a junction outrank cars outside it.
- **Parking lots are one connected space** (2 tiles x 2 bays): a car entering by either gate can park in any of the 4 bays, so a single road to one gate is enough (connecting both is optional). It leaves by whichever connected gate is cheapest. The lot is one mover-at-a-time zone with a wider safety margin; a car about to leave yields to anyone who has claimed it.
- **Deadlock breaking:** every hold records who it waits on. A closed loop of stopped cars gets a short priority override on one member (soft rule holds only; never through a committed car or a physically blocked path).
- **Gridlock:** a car stopped for `gridlock.warnAfterSeconds` gets a gold shine that builds up; at `gameOverAfterSeconds` it's game over (screen arrives in M4). Roundabouts / traffic lights arrive in M5 using the same framework.
- Headless stress test results: 8 houses sharing roads, 60 maps x 240 s: 1,145 junction tiles, 6,000+ trips, 0 car overlaps, worst stall 3.5 s. A deliberately absurd 11-house tangle still showed a rare brief overlap.

## Water
- Bridge: drag a road across water; it auto-builds, straight line only, any length, consumes one bridge item. Inherits 2- or 4-lane from the road being dragged. Bought with money.

## Flow, maps and game over
- Splash ("Tap to Start", unlocks audio) -> main menu (pick a map) -> play. **Esc** / Menu button pauses (resume, new map, main menu).
- Maps live in `config/maps.json` (name + size + unlock rule). Bigger maps unlock by reaching N trips on the previous one; bests are saved in `localStorage`. Terrain features scale with map size.
- **Game over = gridlock:** any car stopped for `gridlock.gameOverAfterSeconds` ends the run (gold shine warns first). Screen shows trips, time survived, cash and best, with Play again / Main menu.

## Progression (config/progression.json)
- **Pacing scales with traffic:** next goal/spawn = trips now + max(min, houses x `tripsPerHouse`), so the rhythm stays steady as houses (and trips/second) grow instead of snowballing. Goals ~every 8 trips per house, spawns ~every 2.5.
- **Spawns** are trip-triggered (`firstAtTrips`, then as above). The first `sameColorSpawns` reuse the starting color; then every `newColorEverySpawns`-th spawn introduces a new color as a destination + house pair. Otherwise a color gets extra houses until it has `maxHousesPerDestination` per destination, then a new destination + house. New buildings land near existing ones, reachable over land within `roadsNeeded` road tiles, never on roads/buildings (an exit may touch an existing road and auto-connect). They pop in with a ring.
- **Goals** every N trips (growing). The reward menu offers `roadsPlain` (30) roads OR `roadsWithSpecial` (20) roads + one random *enabled* special. Specials not built yet are listed with `"enabled": false` and flip on when finished.
- **Cash** (slim, per trip) buys specials that have a `cost` in the Shop (bridge $40).

## Bridges
- Drag from a shore road straight across water to the far bank: one bridge item covers any length (even one tile), water tiles cost no road pieces, only the far-bank tile does. Straight line only; inherits 2-lane for now. Demolishing any span tile removes the whole bridge and refunds the item; cars on it keep going on a ghost.

## Progression (older notes)
- Every X total trips: a **goal** is hit -> reward choice: 20 plain pieces + one special, OR 30 plain pieces. Always offered at least 20 pieces.
- Spawns after thresholds. First couple of spawns reuse the starting color; then new colors (new color always spawns with a matching house).
- Every completed round trip pays a **slim** amount of money (game is slightly greedy). Money buys specials not offered by goals (e.g. bridges).
- Colors: 4 at first, colorblind-safe (Okabe-Ito) + shape glyphs. Defined in `config/colors.json`.

## Specials
Highway (10 four-lane pieces, still to build), roundabout, traffic light, tunnel (through raised land), one-way (toggle), ramps (count as 1 plain piece each; exit perpendicular or straight ahead as overpass). Overpass needs two layers in a tile - the only exception to one-type-per-tile; scrap it if too costly.

## Game over
- If **any car** sits stuck too long (generous timer), the game ends. Warning: stuck car / area pulses with a gold shine that intensifies. Then: restart or main menu.
- Maps: fixed size per map; bigger maps at higher levels. A map is played until loss, quit or no room left (TBD).

## Audio
- Splash screen ("Tap to Start") unlocks the Web Audio context. `js/audio.js` exposes `unlock()`, `play(name)`, mute. Register sounds in its `SOUNDS` map; files go in `assets/sounds/`.

## Fix round after M6 (playtest feedback)
- **Shop** is a bottom sheet above the toolbar (never covers Shop/Menu buttons) with its own Close button; border-box sized so it fits narrow phones.
- **Green-light queues**: the no-box-blocking rule ignores a leader that is rolling (v > 1 and gap > 0.22); only a (nearly) stopped leader counts as spillback, so a platoon flows through a green.
- **Roundabouts** yield only to cars already circulating or about to enter (within 0.3 of the line), never to cars merely approaching; a car whose merge point the ring car has cleared falls in behind it.
- **Spawn budget**: `buildCost()` (0-1 BFS, existing roads free) makes new houses/pairs spawn only where the player's held road inventory can connect them. Still TODO: houses do not yet get impatient when unconnected.

## Driveways (house legs) - rules agreed in RT
- A house's connection (and a destination gate, same rule) never counts toward the junction type: house + road + house across the street is a plain through road, not a 4-way. Cars leaving a house always yield to street traffic and don't stop first (`houseSides` in `junctionAt`).
- Not yet built (future, user-approved shape): a **driveway toggle** piece. Costs a road piece, serves only the one house it starts at, cannot connect to a destination, and the moment it touches a road it stops being a driveway. Drawing a road across a driveway is blocked with a toast. Houses that get angry from long waits are fixed by rotating the house - some things are meant to suck.
- Destination gates now yield like driveways. Reason (playtest): a destination has 2 adjacent exits and lights can't be adjacent, so a light on one exit was moot. Alternative if yield isn't enough: give destinations a single exit.
- **Destinations now have ONE exit** (lot tile 0 only; the second lot tile is parking). Combined with gate-yield this removes the "two adjacent exits make a light moot" problem. `LOT_GATES` in `js/buildings.js`; spawn checks, planBack and drawing follow it.
- **Fallback if single-exit lots bottleneck (user idea, not built):** let cars phase through each other while on the destination's property (lot pieces only); street and gate rules unchanged.

## Economy change: roads cost cash (playtest: 133 spare roads, plenty of cash)
- Goals no longer give roads. A goal offers **special + $20** or **just $80** (`goals.cashWithSpecial` / `cashPlain`); if no special is available it auto-grants the cash.
- A 2-lane road piece costs `economy.roadCost` ($2) once the spare stock runs out. Spare stock = the 20 starting pieces + anything refunded by demolishing (refunds still instant, still go to stock). 4-lane pieces still come from Highway grants.
- The road pill shows `roadsAvailable` = stock + floor(cash / cost). Spawn budgets use the same number.
- Cash is still plentiful ($5/trip); raise `roadCost` or lower `rewardPerTrip` if road never feels like a decision.

## Known issues (open)
- **Car parked in a bay and never leaving** (user screenshot, full map, all houses connected, not game-ending). Not reproduced as an isolated case. In bot runs every long-parked car had a local traffic deadlock next to its lot (cycles among `outranked` / `chain-inside` / `chain-earlier` / `box` / `stop` waits that `breakDeadlocks` does not resolve, e.g. a car held at `stop` that never reaches the line because of a stopped leader). That deadlock class already existed at M6 (bot: 6-8 of 10 games ended in gridlock at 25 min). Parked cars never count as "stuck", so they never glow or end the game.
- Ideas if it shows again: make a parked car that has waited too long for its lot to clear flash gold like a stuck car; extend `breakDeadlocks` to treat `box` / `stop` waits as part of a cycle.

## Rotating a destination with cars heading in / parked (playtest bug, fixed)
- Rotating a destination used to leave its cars on the old lot geometry; once the old exit road was changed they could never leave. Now: parked cars are re-seated in their bay on the new layout immediately (`reseatCarsOf`), and cars still driving in finish their route (ghost rule) and are re-seated on arrival. A re-seated car leaves through the new gate and waits in its bay if the new gate has no road yet.

## Gridlock patience ladder (playtest: "nobody moves at the green light", snowballing backups)
- Reproduced with a lit grid: a handful of cars end up inside two neighboring junctions each waiting for the other's tile (or for room beyond), and the jam grows until the map locks. Roundabouts and plain grids showed it too.
- A car that has been stuck > 0.8 x `gridlock.warnAfterSeconds` while waiting on **other cars** (chain-inside / chain-earlier / box / outranked / chain-light) now stops deferring to those rules (`force`); after 1.3 x it also ignores every car inside the box (`ignoreAll`). Red lights and stop-sign timers are never skipped. A brief overlap in a jam is accepted over a permanent deadlock.
- Not fixed (by design): a genuinely full ring of roads with no free space anywhere is still a gridlock; that is what the stuck-car game over is for.
- Sim results: lit 4x4 grids, 20-60 houses: before, most runs had cars stuck for 500-1500 s; after, 14 of 15 runs clear (worst wait 20-33 s). Bot (full progression): games over 6-8 of 10 -> 0 of 10, three runs.

## Cut / Join tool (user request)
- Toolbar button **Cut** (key `c`). Drag across the seam between two touching road tiles to cut it; the first seam of the drag decides whether the whole drag cuts or rejoins (drag again over a cut seam to rejoin). A tap shows a hint toast.
- A cut seam is a wall in both directions (`road.cut` bitmask on both tiles; `roadConns` skips cut sides, so pathfinding, junction types, lights, roundabouts and drawing all follow). Cars already on a route across it finish as ghosts, then replan. One-way flags on that seam are cleared when it is cut.
- Not allowed on bridges, tunnels and overpass tiles; not needed at house/destination entrances (they connect only to their own road). Cuts reset when either tile is demolished/rebuilt. Free to use. Drawn as a dark curb with yellow dashes across the road end.

## Lot gate flip (user request)
- A destination's single gate is lot tile `b.gate` (0 or 1, default 0). **Tap the building = rotate; tap the parking lot = flip the gate to the other end of the lot** (`flipLotAt`). Parked cars are re-seated in their bay on the new layout; cars still driving in finish their ghost route and are re-seated on arrival (same rule as rotation, now also checking the gate). Spawning, pathing and drawing all read `gateOf(b)`.

## Patience ladder, part 2 (playtest: gridlock where a highway meets other roads)
- Reproduced with a lit/unlit grid whose middle row and column are 4-lane: whole stretches of bumper-to-bumper cars where the held cars were *creeping* (so the stuck timer kept resetting) and the cars they waited on were committed-but-stationary (so "forcing" never overrode them).
- Fix: a per-car patience clock `holdT` (runs while held, decays when free, ignores creeping); the ladder uses `max(stuck, holdT)`. A forced car now also stops yielding to an outranking car that is committed but nearly stopped (v < 0.3).
- Sim: 4-lane grids, 6 seeds each: before, 1-2 of 6 runs jammed for 400-1000 s; after, 24 of 24 clear (worst wait 14-35 s; a single car >15 s at the end in one run). Tessa's sim (`sim/run.mjs`, 3 maps x 24 games): still 0% gridlock, mean riverbend trips 700 -> 741, warnings 8.2 -> 5.0.

## Save games (user request)
- `js/save.js`: `serialize()` / `restore()` turn the whole game into plain JSON and back: terrain grid, roads (incl. one-way / cut / bridge / tunnel / lights / roundabouts / overpasses), buildings (gate, lot slots), progress (goals, spawn schedule, colors), inventory/cash/unlocks, a pending goal reward, and **every car mid-trip** (state, exact position/speed, lot bay, and its route stored as piece keys, rebuilt with `pieceFromKey`). A refresh therefore brings the jam back instead of clearing it.
- Storage is pluggable: `backend = { load, save, remove, list }` (sync or async). `localBackend` (localStorage) is the default; `httpBackend({ baseUrl, headers })` is a ready server adapter (GET/PUT/DELETE `{baseUrl}/{slot}`, tested against a fake server). Switch with `setBackend(...)` in `js/main.js`; `config/save.json` holds backend id, slot name and autosave interval (10 s).
- Autosave every 10 s while playing, plus on page hide / unload, and when going to the main menu. Game over deletes the save. The menu shows a **Continue** card (map, trips, minutes) when a save exists. Save version 1; unknown versions are refused instead of half-loaded.
- Tests: `npm run test:save` (round trip identical, cars return in the same spot, game keeps running, reward screen survives, both backends).

## Sim additions
- `node sim/run.mjs --bot flip,cut` turns on two bot behaviours that use the newer mechanics: **flip** (flip a destination's gate if that makes a color's first hookup cheaper) and **cut** (every 30 s, try cutting a seam between two neighboring junctions; keep it only if every connected house still has a route). Summary line shows flips / cuts kept / cuts undone.
- Speed-ups (results bit-identical to before on 180 checked games, incl. cuts/flips/resave; ~2-2.5x faster): junction info is cached across steps (`junctionCache`, dropped when `netVersion` changes: `emit()` in state.js bumps it, and spawns/restores replace `g.ports` / `g.roads`); `pieceConflict` geometry is worked out once per pair of pieces; pieces carry their space key (`tk`), a unique `uid` and per-step occupancy (`occ`); the pathfinder reuses its arrays; `g.headless` (set by the sim) skips car position/heading maths that only drawing uses. **Keep the rule: anything that changes roads, specials, cuts, one-ways or cones must go through a state.js function that calls `emit()`**, or the junction cache goes stale. A bigger timestep was tried and rejected: at 1/30 s trips nearly doubled with lights and the roundabout gain flipped sign, so junction behaviour depends on the 1/60 step.
- `--resave 60` serialises and restores the whole game every 60 simulated seconds (as if the player refreshed the page) to check saving doesn't change outcomes.
- Results (3 maps x 24 games, 20 min): baseline, `--bot flip,cut` and `--resave 60` all 0% gridlock; trips within noise of each other (lakeside, 80 games: 775 vs 746 with resave, ~1 SE). Flips are rare in practice (0-0.1 per game); the bot keeps ~1-2 cuts per game and rejects ~80 attempts.

## One Overpass for any road size (user request)
- A single **Overpass** item now works on any straight 4-way crossing of two roads of either size (2/2, 4/2, 2/4, 4/4), not only highway-over-street. It reads the sizes off the touching roads; no S/L choice. Needs 4 roads meeting, the same size on both sides of each road, and no light/roundabout on the tile.
- **Default top road:** the bigger one (a tie puts east-west on top). **While the Overpass tool is on, tapping an overpass swaps which road is on top** (free; cars on the tile finish as ghosts). The tool stays on after the last piece is placed so it can still be tapped to swap; the Overpass pill also shows (count 0) whenever one exists on the map.
- Implementation: `r.overpass` = axis of the deck (0 N-S, 1 E-W); `r.lanes` = deck's lane count; per-layer lane counts come from `layerLanes()` (network.js), which `edgeLanes`, car routes and `pieceValid` use. Renderer draws a 2- or 4-lane deck over a 2- or 4-lane road (`drawWide` takes a mid half-width and a rails flag).
- Reward offer needs a crossing to exist (`requires: "crossing"`). Cost stays $80. `tests/overpass.test.mjs` (`npm run test:overpass`) covers all four size combos, swapping, placement rules and traffic.

## Cutting bridges (user request)
- Bridges can still meet roads/bridges from the side (e.g. two parallel spans), as real ones do, and now the Cut tool works on those seams. A bridge or tunnel can be cut only **across its side**, never **along its length** (that would just break the span); overpass tiles still can't be cut. Spans remember their direction (`axis`, 0 N-S / 1 E-W); older spans derive it from their tile list. Test: `tests/bridgecut.test.mjs`.
- **Sweep cut (side-by-side roads/bridges):** after crossing from one road to a parallel one with the Cut tool, keep dragging ALONG the pair and every step cuts (or rejoins) the seam between them, so a whole parallel run is one drag. It only switches on when the two roads really run side by side; a lone road still cuts along itself as before. Playwright-checked with mouse and touch on 2 parallel bridges and 2 parallel streets (all seams cut in one drag, drag again rejoins).

## Ghost roads last the whole round trip (user rule, bug fixed)
- Rule: a car's ghost road must stay continuous until the car has finished its **entire** trip, not just the way out. Before, a car parked at a destination planned its way home only when it was ready to leave; if the roads had been demolished or cut while it was parked, it sat in its bay forever (never glowing, never counting as stuck) - the "car stuck in the building" mystery.
- Now `planOut` also stores `car.homeRoute` (the way it came, reversed: lot gate, the same tiles in the opposite direction, its driveway). `planBack` still prefers live roads; if none lead home it uses `homeRoute`. Ghost pieces are drawn for parked cars too. The route is cleared when the car gets home or when its lot is rotated/flipped (the old way home no longer fits), and it is part of saved games.
- Test: `npm run test:ghost` (demolish everything / cut / demolish the middle, while parked and while driving back; the car gets home, its bay frees, the trip counts). The old behaviour failed every parked case. `sim-test` was updated: the car now drives home over the ghost road instead of waiting.

## Construction cones (user request)
- Toolbar **Cone** (key `x`, button shows "Cone (n)" while any exist). Tap or drag along road tiles to put cones on them; the first tile decides whether the drag places or removes. Free and unlimited; drawn as a traffic cone with a faint orange tile tint. Saved with the game (`road.cone`); rebuilding a tile starts without its cone.
- A coned tile is closed to **new** trips (`findPath` never starts on or enters one). Cars already on a route keep it, and parked cars whose only way home crosses a cone use their remembered ghost route (`homeRoute`), so a cone never strands a car. Houses with no route simply wait.
- Use case: closing a half-built road so another color doesn't discover it and send traffic over it, without cutting/rebuilding. `tests/cone.test.mjs` (`npm run test:cone`).

## Speed control
`game.speed` is 0 (frozen), 1, 1.5 or 2. The frame loop in `main.js` multiplies elapsed time by it before feeding the fixed 1/60 accumulator, so physics and timers behave identically at every speed. Speed 0 keeps `mode === 'play'`, so all building tools work while frozen; cars, ghost routes and home routes simply resume. HUD pill cycles ⏸ → 1x → 1.5x → 2x; numpad `0` pause, `1` 1x, `2` 1.5x, `3` 2x (number row `1`-`6` pick the toolbar tools left to right: Build, Demolish, Move, Cut, Cone, One-way; letter keys still work). Tapping Space toggles between pause and the last running speed (holding Space and left-dragging pans instead, and then letting go doesn't toggle). New games start at 1x; Continue restores paused. Speed is not saved.

## Settings
`js/settings.js` keeps player preferences in this browser's localStorage (not in save games). Settings screen: main menu or Pause. "Show cut markers" (default on) hides the black bar with yellow dashes drawn at a cut seam; it only affects drawing, never routing. The markers always show while the Cut tool is selected.

## Sound effects
`js/audio.js` plays sounds by event name, listed in `config/sounds.json` . An entry is a file, a list of files, or an object: `{ "files": [...], "volume": 0.8, "pitchJitter": 0.05, "volumeJitter": 0.1, "cooldownMs": 70 }`. With several files, one is picked at random each play and never the same one twice in a row; the jitters nudge pitch/volume a little each time so even a single file doesn't sound identical. Game code calls `play('destroy')`. Files go in `assets/sounds/`. Wired so far: `spawn` (a house or destination appears, also the dev `P` key), `build` (anything a player places: road tiles, widened highway tiles, bridges, lights, roundabouts, overpasses, cones; 6 variants; cuts are silent) `gameover` (sad trombones when the gridlock timer ends the game; 7 variants), `rotate` (a building is rotated by tap or the `r` key, or a destination's lot is flipped by tapping it: cinder block rubbing), `pause` (speed set to the pause: tires screeching to a halt) and speed-change sounds: every speed change emits `speed` {speed, prev} and `main.js` plays the first registered of `speed:<from>><to>` (e.g. `speed:0>1.5`), then the general kind `speed:resume` (from pause) / `speed:up` / `speed:down`. Registered now: `speed:0>1`, `speed:0>1.5`, `speed:0>2` (all the car-starting sound, kept separate so each can get its own later). Slowing down between running speeds: `speed:2>1.5`, `speed:2>1`, `speed:1.5>1` (all the car-downshift sound, kept separate too). Speeding up between running speeds: `speed:1>1.5`, `speed:1>2`, `speed:1.5>2` (all the car-accelerating sound, kept separate too). Every speed move now has a sound; the general `speed:up` / `speed:down` / `speed:resume` hooks are unused fallbacks, `tunnel` (a tunnel is built through a hill: jackhammer drilling, 3 variants; bridges use `build`), `toast` (any toast message that shows; plays alongside whatever else is sounding; an identical message within 1.5 s is suppressed, sound included), `popup` (any element marked `data-popup` in index.html opens: shop, pause, settings; mark a future dev window the same way), `hover` (a mouse first enters any button; touch never plays it), `click` (a button is pressed, mouse, touch or keyboard; hotkeys that do what a button does, like tool keys, number row, numpad speed, Space and Esc, play it too), `honk` (8 variants; a car that has been stuck long enough to glow honks, every ~5 s at first and every ~1.2 s as it nears game over, a bit louder as it gets madder; `tuning.json` `gridlock.honkSlowSeconds` / `honkFastSeconds`; test: `npm run test:honk`) and `destroy` (a road tile, bridge or special is demolished; 4 variants, 70 ms cooldown so dragging across tiles stays tidy). Hooks live in `main.js` (`onEvent`) and `input.js`. Test: `npm run test:audio`.

Planned: separate volume sliders and mute buttons for effects and music in Settings once music exists (`setMuted` is the single master switch for now).

## Music and volume
- **Songs:** put audio files in `assets/music/` and list them in `config/music.json`: `{ "crossfadeSeconds": 4, "tracks": [ { "title": "Quiet Asphalt", "file": "assets/music/quiet-asphalt.mp3" } ] }`. No tracks = no music and no music button. A track can also be just a file name (the title is made from it).
- **Playback (`js/music.js`):** a shuffled playlist that plays every song once per lap and never opens a lap with the song that just played ("shuffle with no repeat"). Songs crossfade into each other (the next one starts underneath `crossfadeSeconds` before the end). Two `<audio>` elements take turns, routed through the shared AudioContext so volume works on iPhones too. Starts at the splash screen's Start button (browsers need a click). Previous goes back through what was played. The music never stops for game state: neither pause (speed or Menu / Esc), the main menu, reward nor game-over touches it. It plays until the player pauses or mutes it (so the volume sliders in Settings can be heard).
- **Music panel:** the note button in the top bar (only shown when there are songs) opens a small panel: song title, previous / play-pause / next, volume, mute. It plays the popup sound like the other panels.
- **Settings screen:** volume slider and mute for sound effects, and the same for music (the music group is hidden when there are no songs), next to "Show cut markers". The panel and the Settings screen show the same values.
- **Saved:** everything lives in `js/settings.js` (browser localStorage, not in save games): `sfxVolume` (default 100%), `sfxMuted`, `musicVolume` (default 75%), `musicMuted`, `musicOn`. Sliders use a gentle curve (`gainOf`: position squared), so 75% on the slider is about 56% gain: roughly half to three-quarters loudness. Test: `npm run test:music`.

## Two-color destinations
A destination can have a second color (`color2`): same 2x2 shape, still one entrance and 4 shared bays, but cars of EITHER color may park there (`accepts(dest, colorId)` in `buildings.js`; used by car dest choice, spawning and the sim bot). The point is difficulty: houses of two colors that would normally use separate roads all funnel through one entrance.
- **Look:** the building is split into two halves, one per color, each with its own shape (color is never the only cue).
- **Spawning:** only as a "combo" spawn (a new destination because a color's houses outgrew its destinations), only once two colors are in play, and only after `milestones.dual.afterTrips` in `config/milestones.json` (2000) trips. Chance starts at `startChance` (1 in 3) and climbs to `maxChance` (1 in 2) over `rampTrips`. The other three numbers live in `config/progression.json` under `spawns.dual`; `startChance`/`maxChance` of 0 turns the feature off. The second color's first house is placed at least 5 tiles from the first color's house so their routes have to merge.
- **Counting:** a two-color destination counts as a destination for each of its colors when balancing houses per destination. Saves store `color2` (older saves load unchanged). Test: `npm run test:dual`.

## Small destinations (one building, 2 bays)
A small destination is ONE HALF of the normal 2x2 destination box: its building tile, its lot tile and 2 parking bays, with one entrance on that lot tile. The other half of the box is plain free ground, so it fits in gaps a normal destination cannot. All the lot geometry and car-route code is shared with the big one (`lanes.js` is untouched): a small destination just has `small: true` and `half` (which lot tile, 0 or 1), and `buildings.js` derives the rest (`gateOf`, `baysOf` = the 2 bays it really has, `footprint` = 2 tiles, `destWeight` = 0.5).
- **Spawning:** from the very start. Each time a new destination is due, a small one is tried first with chance `spawns.small.chance` (0.25 in `config/progression.json`), and a small one is always the fallback when a big one does not fit. The first destination of a game is always a normal one. Two-color destinations are always normal size.
- **Capacity:** it counts as half a destination when the spawner balances houses per destination, so it gets about half the houses.
- **Player:** tapping a small destination turns it (there is no gate to flip). It turns to the next direction where its new tiles are free; if no direction fits it stays put. Saves store `small` / `half` (older saves load unchanged). Test: `npm run test:small`.

## Best scores and ending a game
- **Live best:** the best trip count per map is kept live (`trackBest` in `state.js`): it goes up the moment a run passes it, so a run that never jams still counts. It is written to storage at most every ~2 s, when the run ends, when going to the main menu and on `pagehide` (`flushBest`). Maps that unlock from a best score (`unlock` in `config/maps.json`) therefore unlock mid-run, with a toast ("New map unlocked: ...").
- **End game:** the Pause screen has an **End game** button. It needs two taps (the second within 3 s, otherwise it resets), so it cannot be hit by accident. It ends the run as "Game ended" (no trombones; that is for gridlock), shows the same stats screen with "New best!" when it applies, and clears the autosave like any game over.
- **Record flag:** a run is a record if it beats the best at the START of the run (`game.bestAtStart`, saved with the game so Continue does not lose it), not the live best. Test: `npm run test:best`.

## Cones: no player tool (for now)
Testers found the Cone tool redundant once round-trip ghosts existed (Demolish already lets cars finish their trip while new trips route around). The toolbar button, the `x` key and the number-row slot are gone (number row is now 1 Build, 2 Demolish, 3 Move, 4 Cut, 5 One-way). The cone itself stays in the game: `road.cone` still blocks routing (`pathfind.js`), is still drawn, saved and loaded, and `setCone` / `hasCone` / `coneCount` in `state.js` remain. Cones in a save are ignored on load (`restore` in `save.js` drops `road.cone`): with no tool to remove one, a cone from an old save would be a permanent roadblock. The road under it stays. Idea under discussion: the game places cones itself as a "road works" late-game pressure event (a road tile closes for a minute or two and traffic has to reroute).

## Entrances on the sides of the lot
A destination's single entrance can be at the front (the road side) or at the ENDS of its lot, so a destination whose front faces a wall can still be reached from the side.
- **Encoding:** `ent = tile + 2 * code` (`buildings.js`): `tile` (0|1) is the lot tile the entrance sits on, `code` 0 = front, 1 = the -T end of the lot, 2 = the +T end (T = a clockwise quarter turn from the front). A big (double) destination has 4 choices: ent 0, 1 (the two front tiles), 5 (+T end) and 2 (-T end). A small one has 3: its front (`half`), its +T side (`half` + 4) and its -T side (`half` + 2). Older saves only have `gate` (0|1) = ent 0|1, so they load unchanged.
- **Tapping the lot** moves the entrance to the next place, CLOCKWISE round the lot (the same way tapping a building turns it): big 0 -> 1 -> 5 -> 2 -> 0, small front -> +T side -> -T side -> front. Places a road cannot reach (water, or a building right outside) are skipped. Turning a building keeps its entrance in the same place on it, so the entrance turns with it.
- **Code:** `entOf`, `entSide`, `entrancesOf`, `exitTile` in `buildings.js`; the ports map records `ent` and the way it faces, so pathfinding and the road network already worked with any side. `lanes.js` has the lot_in / lot_out curves for side entrances (inside a lot cars may overlap, as agreed; leaving still waits for the lot to be clear, in `updateSim`). `render_objects.js` draws the entrance stub wherever it is.
- **Spawning:** a new destination picks its entrance among the places a road can reach: the front half of the time, otherwise any clear one. The first destination of a game always uses the front. The test bot tries every entrance and keeps the cheapest.
- **Tests:** `npm run test:entrances` runs every destination type x rotation x entrance: cars reach every bay and get home, routes are continuous, nobody is stuck.

## Overpasses next to buildings
An overpass used to need roads on all four arms, so it could never sit right next to a building. Now (`overpassFit` in `state.js`, shared by placing and the hover highlight) one end of the road running UNDER the deck may be a building's driveway or a destination's lot entrance. The deck itself can never come from a driveway: the deck is always the way made of two roads, and tapping an overpass again will not swap the deck onto a road that ends at a building (`driveway` message). Refused: a building at one end of BOTH ways (one would have to be the deck), and a way with no road in it at all. When all four arms are roads nothing changes (the bigger road goes over). Test: `npm run test:overpass-driveway`.

## Maps and map size
Maps are lines in `config/maps.json` (`id`, `name`, `cols`, `rows`, `unlock: { map, trips }`): Riverbend 32x22, Lakeside 44x30 (40 trips on Riverbend), Highlands 60x40 (80 on Lakeside), **Metro 100x66** (150 trips on Highlands). The terrain generator scales rivers and hills with the map, so a bigger map still gets proper water, hills and bridges. Measured up to 200x132 (26,400 tiles) with the bot: game logic costs the same as Highlands (it depends on cars and roads, not tiles), saves stay ~20 KB, terrain generation takes 33 ms. The one thing that grows with map size is drawing the whole map zoomed all the way out (~19 ms per frame at 200x132 on a desktop; a phone is several times slower). If a much bigger map is added, cache the static terrain in an offscreen canvas first. The minimum zoom is `max(3, 0.75 x fit)` px per tile so a phone can fit a 100-wide map.

## Accidents
A difficulty event, not a graphics one: after `milestones.accidents.afterTrips` (1000) trips, every so often two cars bump and block their lane (`js/accident.js`, stepped from `tick` in `state.js`).
- **Pick:** two rolling cars (speed >= `minSpeed`) close together on a plain road piece: on the same piece, or the rear one just behind the front one on its next piece (`closeTiles`). Never in a junction, lot, driveway, overpass, tunnel, bridge or on a light/special tile.
- **Wreck:** both stop dead (`car.crash`, handled in `updateSim`), the front car is knocked to a slant, amber hazards flash, `accident` event (toast + `crash` sound: five recordings in `assets/sounds/crash1..5.mp3`, one picked at random each time). Followers queue behind them through the normal leader-gap logic. The cars' patience keeps running (the wreck's own cars are held at 0), so it can anger a queue and a 15 s stall adds to any jam already building. It does NOT stop the gridlock clock: that was a deliberate choice.
- **Clear:** after `durationSeconds` (20 game seconds, so it scales with game speed) the cars carry on and the police fade out (`drawAccident` in `render_objects.js`, two police cars with red/blue bars). `cleared` event.
- **Schedule:** the first one comes `firstDelaySeconds` to twice that after the threshold is passed (also after Continue: the schedule is not saved and a save made mid-accident loads without the wreck). After each, the next is `minGapSeconds`..`maxGapSeconds` later, shrinking by up to `rampShrink` over `rampTrips` more trips. One at a time.
- **Tests:** `npm run test:accident`.

## Stadium and the outside (the trip ladder)
**One file for the ladder:** `config/milestones.json` holds every "after N trips" number (accidents 1000, two-color destinations 2000, stadium 3000, side roads 4000) and the maps the stadium may appear on. Code reads `milestones.<name>` (`js/progression.js`), `sim/run.mjs` takes `--set milestones.stadium.afterTrips=200`. To add a new feature to the ladder: a new key there and a read in code. The other numbers (chances, durations, intervals) stay in `progression.json` / `tuning.json` under the same names (`spawns.dual`, `spawns.portals`, `accidents`, `stadium`, `outside`).
- **Stadium (3k trips):** one 4-wide, 3-deep oval arena (`stadium: true`; `footprint` in `buildings.js`), with `tuning.stadium.gates` gates (2 to start, taken from `gateTiles` = front-row tiles -1, 2, 0, 1; raise the count for more). A gate on front tile i is entrance `ent = 8 + i + 1` (`SENT`, `stadiumEnt`, `entTile`); each is its own port, its own "space" for the junction logic (`lotSpace`), so several cars can go in and out at once. Only on the maps in `milestones.stadium.maps` and only once every color is in play. It takes ANY color: it is not counted as a destination for any color (no effect on spawning, serving or the bot's connected-house test), but a house car heads for it now and then (`stadium.houseChance`). There is no lot: `stadium.capacity` virtual bays (`bayPiece`), cars park out of sight in the lot tile of the gate they came in by and stay `stayMinSeconds`..`stayMaxSeconds`. A car picks the gate that is cheapest to reach counting the cars already heading for it (`gateQueuePenalty`), so a crowd spreads over the gates the player has connected. Placed (`spawnStadium`) on free land near the existing roads, with room for a road at every gate. If there is no free room anywhere (a map covered in roads) `spawnStadiumClearing` takes the 5x4 spot with the fewest plain road pieces in it, never one with a building, bridge, tunnel or overpass, clears the whole 5x4 area around it (the 4x3 footprint, the row in front where the gate roads arrive, and one extra column; `clearRoadTile` in `network.js`; the pieces go back into the player's stock) and builds on it; the toast says how many pieces it took. If even that finds nothing, it razes buildings too (`razeBuilding` in `cars.js`: a house goes with its car, a destination's cars go home; houses are cheaper to lose than destinations, a destination is never razed if it is the last one of a color that still has houses, and a side-road gate is never razed) and the toast calls the stadium a donation. So a stadium always appears.
- **Side roads (4k trips):** gates (`kind: 'portal'`, one-tile house-like buildings on the map border facing inward; `spawnPortal`) come in WAVES listed in `config/milestones.json` (`portals.waves`: each `{ afterTrips, count }`; the default is two gates at 4000 trips on two different random edges and two more at 5000 on the other two edges; delete the second entry for only two, add entries for more waves). A wave only comes once the stadium exists, and each edge holds at most one gate. A gate is only placed where the player could still lay a road to the stadium, and where the tile in front of it is free land with somewhere to go (`exitHasRoom`: no boxed-in gate). If an edge has no such tile (lined with roads, say) `spawnPortalClearing` takes the cheapest border tile: a plain road piece is cleared (refunded), else a building is razed (houses first; never a stadium or another gate; never the last destination of a color that still has houses). If even that finds nothing the LAST RESORT takes whatever is cheapest, including a destination of any color and a building standing on the tile in front of the gate: the gate always happens (unless the edge is all water). A wave that places fewer gates than it is due (`count`) owes the rest: `spawnOutsideThings` keeps retrying every 10 trips for the missing gates until the due number of gates exists. The gate tile itself is the "road to another city": it carries a skyline icon, Demolish refuses it (toast: "That's the road to another city. It can't be demolished."), tapping it explains it ("This is an expansion to another city you can't see."), it never turns, and the stadium never razes it (`gate-info` events).
- **Visitors (`js/outside.js`):** once a gate reaches the stadium, cars of any color (`car.outside`, no house, unique id) leave a random connected gate for the stadium, stay, drive back and vanish (no score; `outside.rewardPerTrip` cash). The gap between visitors is `startIntervalSeconds` x `decayPerMinute`^(minutes since the first visitor), never below `minIntervalSeconds`, each gap jittered +-30%: it shortens a little every minute, with no steps. They count for gridlock like any car. This is a ramp with no ceiling by design: sooner or later it out-runs any layout. Raise `minIntervalSeconds` (or `decayPerMinute` towards 1) for a gentler end.
- **Hordes (`outside.horde`):** `firstAfterMinutes` (5) after the first visitor, then every `everyMinutes` (a random 5 to 10 minutes), a game is "hosted" at the stadium and a horde launches `size` visitors one after another `spacingSeconds` apart (toast: "A game is being hosted at the stadium!"): `sizeStart`, growing by `sizeGrowth` each time up to `sizeMax`. `houseShare` (0.3) of each crowd are the player's own house cars (idle ones sent to the stadium, `sendHouseCarToStadium`), the rest come from the gates. If the stadium is full or the roads will not take them, the rest give up after `giveUpSeconds` (+1.5 x the planned launch time). The trickle carries on alongside.
- **Saves:** gates and the stadium are ordinary buildings (colors `stadium` / `outside` in `SPECIAL_COLORS`; a stadium saved before it had gates is migrated), visitors are saved with their color, and the visitor clock and horde state are saved with the game.
- **Tests:** `npm run test:stadium` (geometry, spawn rules, gates, visitors, gate sharing, hordes, saves). The bot (`sim/bot.js`, `connectGates`) lays road from each side-road gate to the stadium; `node sim/run.mjs --set milestones.stadium.afterTrips=200 --set milestones.portals.afterTrips=300` stress-tests it (the record has `outside: { stadium, gates, visitors }`). Not yet tuned against real play.

## Patience: connections have deadlines (`js/deadlines.js`)
Everything the player has to keep connected is a **watch** with a clock: a house (needs a road to a destination of its color), a destination (needs a road from a house of each color it takes; a two-color one needs both), the stadium (needs houses of every color in play to reach it), and the side-road gates (ONE shared watch per wave: every gate must reach the stadium and a destination of every color). Unconnected, a watch runs `graceSeconds` silently, then `warnSeconds` of warning (a toast that goes in the history, the `unserved` sound, a pulsing red ring with the seconds left on the building, and for the stadium/gates a countdown chip), then **game over** (`Out of patience!`, naming what was not connected). 60 + 60 for all of them to start with (`tuning.deadlines`; houses and ordinary destinations share the `house` numbers). Connected again, a watch resets. A connection that breaks later starts the whole countdown over with a toast ("... is no longer connected"; a plain toast, not logged, because roads are being edited: the building's own warning is what ends up in the history if it stays broken).
- **How "connected" is decided:** `reachablePorts` in `pathfind.js` is one flood fill per house / gate with the same movement rules as `findPath` (one-way streets, overpass layers, cones), so a check is a handful of flood fills, not a search per pair. `tuning.deadlines.requiredPercent`: 0 = at least ONE of each color must be connected; any other number = that percentage (rounded up, at least one) of each color's houses (for the stadium) or destinations (for a gate). Meant to be raised later for a difficulty setting or a sandbox.
- **Not on every edit:** checks wait until the roads have been left alone for `quietSeconds` (0.4 s, game loop) or 450 ms (the HUD, so chips update while building paused), so dragging a long line of road or demolishing one is not a check per tile. A connection only counts as BROKEN after `settleSeconds` (3 s) of no edits.
- **Popups:** the stadium and each wave of gates raise a popup (game mode `notice`, everything stopped) explaining the rule; the clock starts at [OK] (`ackNotice`). A popup that was waiting is waiting again after a load. Clocks only run while the game runs: pause (or a popup) stops them, and you can build while paused.
- **Jammed roads can't be demolished:** a road tile with a blinking (stuck at least `gridlock.warnAfterSeconds`) car on it refuses Demolish (toast "Can't demolish this: cars are jammed here"), because pulling the road out sends every car onto a ghost track and rebuilding "solves" the jam. A bridge or tunnel is refused as a whole if any tile of it has such a car.
- **Tests:** `npm run test:deadlines`.

## Message history (the Log button)
`game.log` (saved with the game, newest last, capped at 200): every toast that is about something goes through `note()` in `hud.js` (toast + `addLog`), with the tile boxes it is about. The Log panel lists them newest first; tapping an entry closes the panel and `flyTo` (`camera.js`) pans and zooms so every box is in view, then a highlight frame pulses over them for about 4 seconds (`drawHighlight`). New spawns, the stadium, gates, hordes and patience warnings link to their buildings; a traffic jam is logged once when its first car starts to blink (`js/jams.js`: blinking cars within `jams.radiusTiles` are one jam, over after `forgetSeconds` without one, and the same spot is not reported again for `relogSeconds` after that: all in tuning.json); an accident links to where it was (drawn faint and dashed if it has already cleared). The countdown chips (stadium / gates) also fly to their buildings when tapped.
- **The match:** from kickoff (the horde starts) cars that arrive stay parked (hidden, but they are real cars: still in `game.cars` with their destination and bay, saved with the game) until the final whistle, `stadium.matchSeconds` (60) after the last one has been launched. At the whistle (`whistle` event: "The game is over!" in the history, and `play('crowd')`: four cheering-crowd recordings, `assets/sounds/crowd1..4.mp3`, one at random) everyone inside leaves, staggered over `exitSpreadSeconds` (10), each by a RANDOM free gate with a road home (`planBack` takes the gate; the car re-emerges from that gate's lot tile). Outside a match, visitors stay `stayMinSeconds`..`stayMaxSeconds` as before. A car that arrives after the whistle is not part of the match.

## Keyboard bindings (Settings > Keyboard)
Every hotkey is an ACTION with up to two keys (`config/keybinds.json` has the defaults; `js/keys.js` is the model; `ACTION` in `input.js` says what each action does). Actions: Build, Demolish, Move, Cut, One-way, Bridge, Tunnel, Highway, Overpass, Roundabout, Traffic light, Rotate building, Shop, History log, Music, and speed Pause / 1x / 1.5x / 2x. Defaults: 1/B, 2/D, 3/M, 4/C, 5, G, T, H, V, O, I, R, S, L, N and numpad 0-3. Bridge and Tunnel pick the Build tool with a hint toast (they are not tappable pills); the pill actions click the pill, so they only work while the pill is showing and press again to put the tool away. Space (hold to pan, tap to pause) and Escape (close the topmost window, else pause) are fixed, so are Tab, Enter, Backspace and F-keys; Ctrl/Alt/Meta combinations are ignored by the game so browser shortcuts keep working.
- **Tokens:** a letter is its lower-case character (follows the keycap on other layouts); anything else is the key's code (`Digit1` number row, `Numpad1` numpad, `Slash`...). Digits and numpad keys are different keys on purpose.
- **Rebinding:** Settings > Keyboard opens the list; click a key box and press a key (replaces the first key, keeps the second) or two keys together within 1.5 s (replaces both). Backspace unbinds, Esc cancels the capture. A key that is already another action's asks "Swap?": the clashing key goes to this action and the other action gets one of this action's old keys, so nothing is lost. Changes are saved at once in the browser (`settings.keys`, localStorage; per browser, not in save games); Reset to defaults clears them. Esc closes the Keyboard window (and Settings after it).
- **Tooltips** on the toolbar, the pills and the HUD buttons show the current keys (`titleFor`).
- **Dev keys** (P spawns a pair; Shift + numpad dev speeds on localhost) stay outside the table.
- **Tests:** `npm run test:keys` (the model) and a browser check of the whole panel.

## Growth clears roads (no filling the map to stop the game)
If a map is packed with road, new houses and destinations find no free land and the game would never get harder. So when spawning has failed for `spawns.makeRoom.afterTrips` (25) trips, `makeRoomForGrowth` (`spawn.js`) clears the road pieces in the `size` x `size` (7) patch near a destination that has the fewest of them (no building in it, never a bridge / tunnel / overpass; the pieces go back to the player's stock), says so ("The city is growing...", in the history, flies to the spot) and the next spawn uses the room. The stadium (`spawnStadiumClearing`) and the side-road gates (`spawnPortalClearing`) do the same for themselves, and razing buildings is their last resort. The "no room since" counter is reset when a game starts.


### Buildings stay off the edge
`spawnPair` and `spawnHouse` first try with a 2-tile margin from the map edge (`EDGE` in spawn.js) and only fall back to the whole map when nothing fits. That leaves the border free for stadium/side-road gates. Bridge/tunnel ("across") spawns are not affected.

### Light gap-out and 40 s accidents
A light's green gives way when the cross street has demand and either (a) the green axis has had no demand for `trafficLight.gapOutSeconds` (2 s, after the 2 s minimum green) or (b) the green has run `maxGreenSeconds` (10 s) while still busy. Both axes use the same rules (`stepLight` in signals.js; `sig.gap` is the quiet time). Accidents last `accidents.durationSeconds` (40 s). The queue behind a wreck counts like any other jam (warning, jam log), but it can never reach `gridlock.gameOverAfterSeconds` (40 s) before the wreck clears, so an accident alone can't end a game. Whatever happens after it clears is up to the player.

### Jam lock covers the tile ahead
`jammedTiles` (cars.js) locks the tile under every car stuck for `warnAfterSeconds` and the next road tile on its route (the one it is waiting to enter; with no route left, the tile it is facing), so the junction a queue is waiting for can't be deleted to dissolve the jam. Roads beside or behind it stay free, and the lock lifts the moment the car moves.

### Road works
Shares the accident schedule (`stepAccidents`, accident.js): when the timer fires it is a coin flip between an accident and road works (works only from `milestones.construction.afterTrips`, 1500). The pick sticks and is retried every 2 s; after `accidents.switchAfterSeconds` (30) the other kind steps in. One event at a time (`g.accident` / `g.work` are separate slots, so allowing one of each later is a small change).
- **Where:** `pickSpot` (construction.js) looks at the pieces cars are about to drive and takes a plain straight two-way 2-lane tile nobody is on, so the closed side (`closedIn`, either direction) always has traffic coming. Busier roads come up more often.
- **How cars cross:** `workPiece` (lanes.js, key `rw:x,y:in`) is the car's own lane swung across the center line and back, with the same ends as the plain piece. `adoptWork`/`releaseWork` (cars.js) swap the pieces ahead of every car when the works start and end; new routes are swapped at planning. The tile is flagged a junction (`junctionAt`, `g.work`), so the ordinary hold/priority rules (inside, first come first served, patience ladder) make the two directions take turns.
- **Timers:** the length is clamped under `gridlock.gameOverAfterSeconds`, so a queue can't end the game before it clears. The jam toast and area clock work exactly as for any jam. New trips pay `construction.pathPenalty` to route through the tile. A save drops the works (like accidents); cars keep their saved `rw:` pieces.
The tiles under an accident's wrecks and the road-works tile are locked against demolishing too (`sceneTiles` in cars.js, checked in `demolish`; the toast says why). Cutting, rotating and building are still allowed. The lock ends when the event clears.

### Stadium gate lanes
A stadium's parked cars are out of sight, so its gate pieces are plain straight lines in the right-hand lane (`lotInPiece` / `lotOutPiece` with `ent >= 8` in lanes.js, flagged `gate`), not the curve into one of the lot's two physical bays. Two gate pieces get no extra parking-lot conflict margin (`pairGeometry`, junction.js), so an exiting car doesn't hold up one going in. The gate's busy/claimed rule for leaving cars is unchanged.

### Lanes follow the turn (4-lane roads)
`roadPieces` (cars.js) gives every step of a route its lane: a right turn uses the outer lane (1), a left turn the inner lane (0). A car moves into the lane for its next turn as early as it can (the first straight tile after its previous turn) and a turn leaves it in the lane it turned into. The random inner/outer pick only applies to a run with no turn in it. 2-lane edges (and the edge to a building) always collapse to the one lane. `tests/lanes.test.mjs` runs a stadium on a highway in all four rotations.

### Lights on bridges
`placeSpecial` allows `light` (only) on a bridge tile, and only where it already has 3 or more connections (a road meeting the span from the side). Nothing else changes: the junction/light logic is per road tile and a bridge tile is a normal road entry with a `bridge` flag. Demolishing a bridge returns any light on its tiles (`demolish`, state.js). Roundabouts, overpasses and everything on tunnel tiles stay blocked. `tests/bridgelight.test.mjs` covers placement, cycling and the refund.

### Where visitors go
A steady visitor from a side-road gate (`car.outside`, not `forceStadium`) picks at random among every destination with room that takes its color, plus the stadium (`planOut`, cars.js). A match crowd (`stepHorde` in outside.js, `launchOutside(..., stadiumOnly = true)`) and house cars sent to a match still go to the stadium only. If the stadium is full, visitors go to the other destinations instead of waiting. They return to their gate when done, as before.

### Parked ideas for difficulty levels (not built)
- **Per-gate visitor schedules.** Today all side-road gates share one visitor clock (`g.nextOutsideAt`, `visitorInterval`) and one cap (`tuning.outside.maxVisitors`); each launch picks a random connected gate. A toggle could give each gate its own timer so more gates means more traffic.
- **A second car for some houses (built).** `tuning.houses.secondCarChance` (0.1) is the chance a new house gets a second car (`addCarsFor`, cars.js, used by every house spawner). The second car has its own id (`g.nextId++`; saves already keep `id` and `house` apart), makes its own trips (each counts) and waits at home while its sibling pulls out of the shared driveway. Set the chance to 0 for none. Still open as a difficulty toggle: none at easy levels, more later.

### Parked: difficulty panel and star rating (not built)
A Difficulty section in Settings made of toggles, each a named bundle of config overrides with a weight; the stars (1 to 5) come from the summed weights of the toggles that are on, mapped through thresholds in the same file (`config/difficulty.json`: id, label, weight, overrides; some toggles with levels such as off / some / a lot). Today's game is the 3-star baseline, so easier toggles are needed too (no accidents, gentler jam clock). Locked at the start of a run; the stars are stored with the run and shown beside the best score. Candidate toggles so far: second-car chance (`houses.secondCarChance`), per-gate visitor schedules, accident and work-zone frequency, how fast the visitor gap shrinks, match crowd size, patience and jam clock strictness, bridge and overpass supply.

### Parked: graphics quality and frame-time readout (not built)
- **Quality pill/setting: High / Low / Auto**, saved with the other settings (`roadwarrior.settings`). Low: cap the canvas pixel density at about 1.5 (it is `min(devicePixelRatio, 3)` today), skip the soft extras (shadows, glow around police lights and the stadium, fades, extra cone and lane detail), optional 30 fps cap. The simulation is untouched, so a Low game plays out exactly like a High one.
- **Auto:** time the gap between animation frames; if the typical frame time stays high (around 25 ms) for a sustained stretch, step down to Low with a toast. It only ever steps down by itself, and it ignores the first seconds after loading, pauses and menus, a hidden tab, and single hitches. It should act only when drawing is the slow part, so the readout below has to time drawing and simulation separately. Battery-saver (30 fps) will read as slow, which is fair.
- **Frame-time readout:** a dev pill showing drawing and simulation cost in milliseconds, so phone tests give numbers.
- **Terrain cache for very large maps** (already parked) is the first thing to build if Metro or a larger SuperMetro stutters.
- Testing note: the S26 Ultra is not a representative phone; ask for a mid-range or older device.

### Kick-off waits for the crowd
When the last car of a horde has been launched, the match goes into a `gather` phase (`g.match`, outside.js) for up to `outside.horde.gatherSeconds` (30). It kicks off (`play`, then the whistle after `stadium.matchSeconds`) as soon as no car of the crowd (`car.crowd`) is still driving to the stadium, or when the 30 seconds run out, whichever is first. Cars arriving after kick-off stay parked until the whistle, as before. A save keeps the phase but not the crowd marks, so a game loaded mid-gather kicks off at once.

### Tighter turns: oncoming left turns pass each other
A turn piece is a cubic Bezier whose control points sit a fraction of the way toward the corner where the two lane lines meet (`roadPiece`, lanes.js). That fraction is `tuning.cars.leftTurnTightness` and `rightTurnTightness` (0.45 each; it was 2/3 for both). At 2/3 the two oncoming left turns (E to S and W to N) both swept through the middle and came within 0.11 tiles of each other, so the conflict check (`conflictDistance` 0.205, wider on 4-lane roads) made one wait. At 0.45 they stay 0.25 apart. Left alone it would also have made a left turn clip the right turn from the neighboring street (the swap-streets pair), which is why the right turn is tightened too: with both at 0.45 the only conflict-set change is the two oncoming left-left pairs becoming free, on 2-lane and 4-lane tiles. `tests/lanes.test.mjs` checks it. Values below about 0.42 start to create new left/right conflicts.

### A car that can't enter holds nobody else up
In `analyze` (cars.js), when working out whether car A must wait for a higher-priority car B at the same junction, B is ignored if B is itself held for "no room beyond" (`box`) or the chain rules (`chain-*`) and is not already committed. Before this, a right-turner (outer lane) waited for a straight-on car (inner lane) that could not move because the exit was full, until the patience ladder forced it after about 10 s (on a 4-lane road the two paths count as conflicting). B still enters later and then waits normally for the car that went ahead (the `inside` check). `tests/sidebyside.test.mjs` reproduces it (10.6 s before, 1.0 s after).

### Lanes of one approach don't hold each other up (4-lane roads)
The conflict check (`pairGeometry`, junction.js) uses a wider margin on 4-lane tiles (`conflictDistance` + 0.05 = 0.255) for cars that cut across lanes. Two lanes of the same approach start 0.22 apart, so that margin made an inner-lane left turn conflict with every outer-lane straight car (and, because straight beats left, the left-turner waited for the whole queue to clear). The wide margin now applies only to pieces from different approaches; two lanes of the same approach conflict only if the paths really come within `conflictDistance` (a lane change crosses, so it still does). `tests/sidebyside.test.mjs` covers it.

### Spawn color: random by default, demographics as a difficulty option
`pickSpawnColor` (progress.js) picks the color of the next house: a plain random pick among the colors in play, unless the game's `options.demographics` is on, in which case it is the color with the fewest houses per destination (ties at random), as before. The option is the "Color spawn demographics" checkbox in Settings, Difficulty (`settings.difficulty.demographics`, default off), copied into `game.options` when a game starts and saved with it; a save with no `options` loads as random. The 3-houses-per-destination limit (`maxHousesPerDestination`) still turns an overfull color into a new destination. Why: an orange takeover (about half the houses on a 10,000-trip Metro game) because dual and small destinations count for every color they take. This is the first entry in the Difficulty section, so the difficulty panel and star rating (parked above) can grow from here.

### Hide-the-interface button
`#gui-btn` (bottom right of the HUD, an eye icon, hotkey `gui` = U in config/keybinds.json) toggles `body.gui-off`. In that state CSS hides the top bar, toolbar, countdown chips, toasts and the music, history and shop panels; the button itself stays (faded, crossed out). Popups that need an answer (menu, reward, game over, notices) are overlays outside `#hud` and still show. Not saved: a reload starts with the interface on. Patience warnings still draw on the map itself (`drawPatience`).

### Oncoming straight cars never wait for each other
In `pairGeometry` (junction.js) the wider 4-lane margin does not apply to two oncoming straight pieces that each stay in their own lane (`oncoming`): the two inner lanes are 0.22 apart, inside the 0.255 margin, so they used to conflict and one car waited. `tests/sidebyside.test.mjs` checks the geometry and two real cars at a light and at a stop-sign intersection.

### Save files (export and import)
`Settings > Save file`. Export (`exportSaveFile`, main.js) downloads `rw-<map>-<trips>-trips.json`: `{ kind: 'road-warrior-save', formatVersion, exportedAt, save, best }` where `save` is exactly `serialize()` plus the camera view, and `best` is the per-map best trips. While a game is running it exports the live game (`currentSave`, save.js); in the menu it exports the stored autosave. Import (menu only, because a running game rewrites the autosave every 10 s) reads the file, `readExport` (savefile.js) checks its shape and version (a clear message for each problem: not JSON, wrong shape, newer or older version, unknown map, damaged, too big), and if a save already exists asks Replace / Cancel. Applying it writes the autosave slot (`storeSave`) and raises best scores with `mergeBest` (a score never goes down); the menu's Continue card then shows it. A bare save without the wrapper is accepted too. Only the shape is checked: `restore` rebuilds the game when the player taps Continue. `tests/savefile.test.mjs` covers the file format, and the whole flow was run in a browser (export, a second browser profile, import, confirm, junk file, Continue).

### Parked: drawing speed (measured, not needed yet)
Budget-phone check (headless Chromium, phone-sized view, CPU throttled 3x / 4x / 6x, a synthetic dense Metro: a grid of 1,480 road tiles, 340 houses, 40 destinations, 340 cars of which about 300 moving). The player's own S26 Ultra and a cheaper phone both play a full Metro map smoothly, so none of this is needed now.
- **Simulation is cheap:** about 0.5 ms per step normally and 5 to 9 ms at 6x slower, well inside a 16 ms frame.
- **Drawing is the cost.** The game's own drawing code (measured into a tiny canvas, so no pixel painting) took 10 ms zoomed in and 14 ms zoomed out normally; 36 and 49 ms at 3x; 46 and 70 ms at 4x; 93 and 111 ms at 6x. A budget phone with a map this busy would be roughly 15 to 25 fps, still playable. (The headless browser paints on the CPU, so its end-to-end frame times are pessimistic; a real phone paints on the GPU.)
- **Where the time goes:** zoomed out, about 75% is ground, roads and buildings and 25% is cars; zoomed in, about 63% is cars even though only a few are on screen, because `drawCars` (render_objects.js) draws every car on the map with about five drawing steps each (shadow, body, windshield, glint, outline) and never skips off-screen ones.
- **Input stays responsive:** a key press waits for the current frame, then the game stops stepping at once. Pause latency measured at a median 63 ms normally, 172 ms at 4x and 231 ms at 6x slower, worst 380 ms (these include the pessimistic software painting).
- **If it is ever needed, two invisible fixes:** (1) skip cars outside the view (and the cheap per-car steps when a car is only a few pixels), the big win zoomed in; (2) draw the ground and roads once into an offscreen picture and reuse it until the map changes (the terrain cache already parked above), the big win zoomed out. Re-run the same test afterwards to show the gain. A simpler-car-when-zoomed-out change on its own would help little.
